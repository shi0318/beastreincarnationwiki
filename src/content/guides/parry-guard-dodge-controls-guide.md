Beast of Reincarnation uses one defensive input for both a perfect parry and an ordinary guard. The result depends on timing: press just before impact and Emma parries; press too early and she guards instead. That makes early mistakes survivable, but guard is powered by a limited gauge and cannot be treated like a permanent shield.

This guide uses the shipped-game inputs and behavior documented by Polygon, plus Kotaku's launch-week advice about Emma's early dodge upgrade. It does not claim parry frame data, invulnerability duration, or an input buffer that the cited reporting did not measure.

## Controls at a glance

| Action | PC | Xbox controller | PlayStation controller |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parry / guard | `Q` | `LB` | `L1` |
| Dodge a red attack | `Ctrl` | `B` | `Circle` |
| Open Koo's Bloom Arts | `F` | `Y` | `Triangle` |

The game supports full controller input on Steam and marks a gamepad as recommended. These are Polygon's default inputs for the shipped build; a remapped layout can differ.

For Koo's side of the exchange, including what a successful parry gives him, see the [Koo Bloom Arts, FP and QTE guide](/guides/koo-bloom-arts-qte-guide/).

## Parry and guard use the same input

Press `Q`, `LB`, or `L1` **just before the attack lands**. Polygon reports the following visual and mechanical difference:

- A successful parry negates the incoming hit and produces yellow sparkles around Emma's sword.
- An early input becomes a normal guard; the sword does not show the same yellow parry effect.
- Guarding consumes Emma's entanglement gauge.
- Guarding does not generate Koo's florescence points.
- If the entanglement gauge is empty, holding guard no longer prevents Emma from taking full damage.

Guard therefore works as a timing safety net, not a sustainable strategy. One early input can protect you while costing gauge. Several early inputs in a row can empty the gauge and remove that protection.

## What a successful parry pays for

Parrying is the centre of the combat economy because one correct input does more than stop damage. Polygon's combat guide reports that it:

1. Negates damage from the parried attack.
2. Grants florescence points for Koo's Bloom Arts.
3. Adds to Emma's entanglement gauge.
4. Allows an immediate attack-button **riposte**.

That explains why simply holding guard falls behind. Guard spends the resource that Emma's Blade Arts, Nushi skills, and other systems also need, while parry restores resources and creates a counterattack opening.

You do not need to force a riposte after every parry. In a crowd, the safe result may be repositioning or opening Koo's slowed-time menu. The confirmed fact is that the attack-button response exists; whether it is safe depends on the enemies still moving around Emma.

## Red attacks must be dodged

When an enemy flashes red as an attack begins, Polygon reports that the attack **cannot be stopped by guard or parry**. Dodge shortly before impact instead:

- PC: `Ctrl`
- Xbox: `B`
- PlayStation: `Circle`

A correctly timed dodge creates an opportunity to press attack for a counterattack. The red flash is therefore a rules cue: do not keep testing the parry button against it.

This is different from saying Emma has universal dodge invulnerability against every attack. The cited guide confirms the red-attack response and counter; it does not provide a general invulnerability-frame table.

## Unlock the real dodge roll early

Emma begins with a short side-step dodge. Kotaku's launch tips describe its timing as precise and its travel distance as too short to escape some lunging attacks. The same hands-on report recommends following the **Tracking Shot** path in Emma's skill tree to unlock a full dodge roll early.

That is useful progression advice, but it is not evidence for a specific skill cost or exact unlock level. Check the current skill tree in your build and prioritize the visible roll node if the starting side-step is failing to clear attacks.

The roll does not replace parrying. Story difficulty still expects players to read attacks, dodge, and command Koo, and the resource loop still rewards successful parries.

## A reproducible five-minute practice drill

This routine is an editorial practice method derived from the confirmed controls. It is not hidden frame data.

1. **Find one ordinary enemy near a rest point.** Avoid a boss or a mixed group while learning the cue.
2. **Hold distance and watch one full attack animation.** Do not press anything on the first observation.
3. **Press parry deliberately early.** Confirm that Emma guards and the entanglement gauge falls.
4. **Try again closer to impact.** Look for the yellow sword sparkles and the resource increase.
5. **Press attack once after the confirmed parry.** This tests the reported riposte without adding a long combo.
6. **Wait for a red flash.** Use dodge rather than parry, then test the single attack-button counter.
7. **Stop when the gauge is low.** Reset at the nearby rest point instead of learning from an empty guard bar.

Once that distinction is reliable, use the [elements, weakness, stagger and daze guide](/guides/elements-weakness-stagger-guide/) to understand what the successful hits and parries are doing to the yellow enemy bar.

## Common input mistakes

**Pressing parry too early every time.** This feels safe at first because guard absorbs damage, but it quietly empties entanglement. Move the input closer to impact rather than holding the button.

**Trying to parry a red attack.** The red flash changes the required response to dodge. Repeating the wrong defense will not teach the normal parry timing.

**Opening Koo's menu too late.** The menu slows time rather than stopping it. Create space or open it after a parry instead of during an attack that is already touching Emma.

**Judging dodge only by the starting side-step.** Kotaku's recommendation is specifically to unlock the Tracking Shot dodge roll. If movement feels too short, check that skill path before concluding the dodge system never improves.
