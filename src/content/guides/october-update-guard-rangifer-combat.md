Returning to Beast of Reincarnation after the October 25 update can feel surprisingly different even if your equipment has not changed. Guard now uses a continuous press-and-hold input, bosses have received additional moves and behavior adjustments, and the Rangifer variant has revised hitboxes and perfect guard timing. The practical response is a short practice session before spending valuable resources on a difficult encounter. This guide suggests how to structure that session, distinguish input trouble from positioning mistakes, and rebuild confidence without pretending that one timing cue fits every attack.

## Verify the input before judging the fight
Start in a low-pressure location and inspect the current controls. Find Guard, lock-on, ranged weapon switching, and Entanglement Overdrive. The update adds rebinding support for the last of these and improves conflict warnings for actions that cannot overlap. A binding that felt convenient in an earlier layout can still create an awkward combination during a crowded fight. Check what the menu actually saved, then test the action outside the encounter. Do not assume a familiar button icon means the underlying input behavior is unchanged.

With Guard selected, practice deliberately holding the input rather than repeatedly tapping it as an old habit. Watch the character's response and release it on purpose so you know where the held state begins and ends. Then move, turn, and repeat. The purpose is to separate three events you might otherwise merge together: pressing a button, entering a defensive state, and meeting an enemy's active strike. Understanding the first two events makes it much easier to learn the third without blaming the controller for every missed defense.

## Rebuild timing on an ordinary enemy
Choose a nearby opponent whose approach you can observe without additional enemies entering from behind. Spend the first exchange watching the beginning and end of its attack rather than chasing damage. If you are hit, ask whether you were facing the strike, standing too far away to read it, or holding a different input. Repeating one recognizable move teaches more than surviving several unrelated attacks through frantic dodging. Keep enough space to see the animation and avoid backing into scenery that obstructs the camera.

Once you can identify the attack, add a small punish after a successful defense. End the punish early and return to observation. This is a practice recommendation, not a claim that every enemy permits a particular number of hits. The correct punish is the one you can finish before the opponent becomes dangerous again. If the enemy responds with a different follow-up, shorten the sequence. Progress means making the same decision reliably; a lucky long combo that ends in a trade may teach the wrong lesson.

## Treat revised boss moves as new information
Bosses have new moves and adjusted behavior, so an old recording may no longer describe the entire encounter. Enter your next boss attempt expecting at least one unfamiliar sequence. Watch how the boss begins a string, whether it changes direction, and whether the apparent recovery is followed by another attack. Do not infer a guaranteed opening merely because a pre-update guide shows one. A small notebook entry such as “wait for the extra swing” is more useful than repeatedly trying to force an obsolete punish.

Separate learning attempts from completion attempts. During a learning attempt, focus on seeing the unfamiliar move clearly and reaching its end with enough health to observe the recovery. During a completion attempt, use the openings you already understand and avoid improvising a complicated sequence while under pressure. If a fight feels harder, check whether the change is enemy behavior, your input habit, or a loadout interaction. Changing difficulty, weapon, controls, and tactics simultaneously makes the next result difficult to interpret.

## Recalibrate the Rangifer variant carefully
The update specifically adjusts hitboxes and perfect guard timing windows for the Rangifer variant. This is a reason to relearn the encounter, not evidence of a universal easier or harder setting. Begin at a distance where the relevant strike remains visible and let the attack finish once before committing to a counter. Compare your defensive input with the moment of contact rather than the first dramatic movement. Large creature animations can include preparation and movement that look threatening before the damaging part arrives.

After a failed attempt, identify one cause you can test. If contact appears to miss visually but still hurts, change your position and camera angle before concluding that the timing is impossible. If a defense works sometimes, note whether you approached from a different direction or triggered it during movement. Keep the equipment constant while making that comparison. Once the timing feels readable, reintroduce your normal offensive tools one at a time. This preserves the new timing information instead of burying it beneath an entirely different build.

## Test ranged switching and aim separately
Ranged weapons can now be switched during regular attacks and while in Iaido stance. Try the switch in a safe situation, confirm the selected ammunition in the interface, and only then build it into a combat sequence. The update also corrects a mismatch involving loaded arrow types and ammunition counts under specific inputs. A successful menu switch does not prove that the weapon currently loaded the resource you intended. Make a habit of checking before spending a rare option in a high-pressure opening.

Another fix addresses aim drift while locked on with aim assist disabled. If that describes your setup, retest a stationary target from a clear angle before altering several sensitivity settings. Compare locked and unlocked aiming using the same position, then decide whether the existing configuration is comfortable. Do not copy someone else's sensitivity numbers without considering your device. The useful outcome is that the reticle and selected ammunition behave predictably, leaving you free to concentrate on the enemy instead of fighting the interface.

## Return to progression with a repeatable decision
Finish the session with one ordinary encounter and one serious attempt using the same controls. Hold Guard deliberately, wait for the complete enemy string, choose a short punish, and disengage when the opening ends. If the response remains inconsistent, inspect the binding and the physical input before spending an evening rebuilding your character. A control conflict and an unfamiliar boss pattern require different solutions. Neither is automatically solved by finding a stronger weapon or following a damage ranking.

Keep your next goal specific: make the held input comfortable, read the revised Rangifer strike, or perform one ranged switch without losing track of ammunition. Once that goal works reliably, resume exploration and allow the rest to improve through ordinary play. The patch changes enough details that relearning is sensible, but it does not require abandoning everything you understood about spacing and patience. Clear observation, deliberate inputs, and short reliable openings remain a strong foundation for making the updated combat feel under your control.
