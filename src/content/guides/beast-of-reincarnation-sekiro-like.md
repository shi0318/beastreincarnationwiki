Reviewers often call Beast of Reincarnation "Sekiro-like". The comparison is half right: the parry is central, but the game is built around a companion and elemental weaknesses rather than a posture duel.

## Side by side

| | Beast of Reincarnation | Sekiro |
|---|---|---|
| Core defence | Parry (plus dodges and perfect dodges) | Deflect |
| Resource | Parries build **Flow** for Koo | Deflects build enemy posture |
| Companion | Koo – commanded Bloom Arts and Nushi skills | None |
| Damage plan | Element matching + Flow spending + takedowns | Posture break + deathblow |
| Death | No material or experience cost | Lose half Sen/XP, Dragonrot |
| Difficulty options | Story to Ruthless (one-hit) | None |
| Length | About 20–35 hours | About 30–40 hours |

## Where it feels like Sekiro

- Parrying a boss's whole string (Lacerta's three bites, Erymanth's 5-hit combo) is the best way to win.
- Red glows signal danger – though in Beast of Reincarnation a red glow can mean "parry" (Erymanth's combo) or "dodge" (Erymanth's charge).
- Ruthless mode is closer to Sekiro's lethality than anything in the default game.

## Where it does not

- **Koo** changes everything: you are managing two characters.
- **Elements matter** – fire for Rangifer, shock for Lacerta, acid for Taurus, explosives for Erymanth.
- **Exploration** across 13 open regions with campsites, shrines and collectibles.

Other games reviewers mention: Stellar Blade (parry flow), Black Myth: Wukong (creature design) and Nier: Automata (tone).
