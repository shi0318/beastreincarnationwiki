Entanglement Overdrive draws two kinds of complaints on the Beast of Reincarnation Steam forums: it will not trigger, or it seems to do nothing once it does. Those complaints have two separate causes. The first is the input: on keyboard and mouse the default combination is easy to miss, and for most of the game's life it could not be changed. The second is expectation: Overdrive slows time for a few seconds and does not unleash a special attack, so players who finally trigger it often wonder whether it worked at all. The October 25 update (v1.1.02) fixed the first problem by adding Overdrive to Key Config. This guide covers how to trigger it reliably now, what it actually gives you, and when it is worth spending a full gauge on.

## What Entanglement Overdrive does

Entanglement is Emma's resource, shown as the segmented bar at the bottom of the screen. Successful parries fill it, and it is spent by guarding, by finishing a Blade Art combo, by Nushi skills, and by Overdrive. Polygon's August 3 combat guide describes Overdrive as an ability Emma and Koo can use when the entanglement gauge is full, and it is specific about the result: Overdrive slows time for a few seconds and nothing more. There is no finisher, no damage burst and no cinematic follow-up attached to it.

That matters because it changes how you should judge the button. If you trigger Overdrive and then stand still waiting for something to happen, the window passes and you have simply emptied the bar. Overdrive is a tool for buying reaction time. Its value comes entirely from what you do inside the slowed window: a dodge you could not otherwise make, a heal you could not otherwise fit in, or a clean read of an attack you keep misjudging.

Players have also argued about the exact cost. Polygon's guide says the gauge must be full. In the long-running Steam discussion thread about Overdrive, one player reported on August 7 that two segments were enough to trigger it. Those two claims do not match, and this site has not confirmed either one in a controlled test. The practical approach is to check it on your own save: build the bar to two segments in a quiet area, attempt the input, and note whether anything happens before relying on it in a boss fight.

## Why the input fails on keyboard and mouse

Polygon lists the default inputs as holding Shift and the middle mouse button on PC, or pressing the left and right sticks together on a controller. On a controller this is a familiar "press both sticks" action, and one player in the Steam thread wrote in late October that controller input mapping works well across the board. On keyboard and mouse it has been the subject of complaints since launch week.

The Steam discussion titled "How the f execute this overdrive???" opened on August 6 and was still collecting replies in late October. The most useful advice in it is about timing rather than holding: several players reported that holding Shift and then clicking the middle mouse button did nothing, while pressing both inputs at the same moment, as one quick simultaneous press, worked. If you have been treating Shift as a modifier you hold first, try pressing both keys together instead.

There is a second, less obvious conflict. Polygon's guide notes that commanding Koo to attack an enemy that has not spotted you uses the right mouse button plus the middle mouse button on PC. That means the middle mouse button sits inside two different combinations, and an imprecise press during a busy moment can feel like the wrong action fired, or no action at all. Other players in the thread described the combination as Shift+F on their setups and reported missing it often, with one saying in early October that a failed press tied into lock-on left them no longer focused on the monster.

## What the October patches changed

Two consecutive updates touched this exact problem, so check your version before deciding Overdrive is broken.

v1.1.01, released on October 9, fixed an issue where manually canceling Entanglement Overdrive would toggle target lock-on on or off. That bug explains a specific frustration players described: cancel Overdrive, lose the lock on the monster, then spend the next attack fighting the camera. If you remember Overdrive as "the button that breaks lock-on", that behavior is listed as fixed.

v1.1.02, the ninth update, added Entanglement Overdrive to Key Config with custom rebinding and saving. The same patch improved Key Config warnings so the game alerts you when an assigned button conflicts with actions that cannot overlap; the patch notes give lock-on during ammo slot switching as an example. A player in the Overdrive thread pointed to this line the day after the patch went live. Fictions and Game Freak also stated in the same notes that v1.1.02 is the end of the regular post-launch update cadence, so the current input behavior is what you should plan around.

## How to set up a reliable binding

Open Key Config and find Entanglement Overdrive in its own entry. Pick a key you can press without moving your hand off movement and without overlapping a combat action you use under pressure. Avoid anything already involved in lock-on, ammo switching, guard or Koo's commands. After saving, read any conflict warning the game shows instead of dismissing it, because those warnings were added precisely for combinations that cannot overlap.

Then test it outside combat. Build the gauge with a few parries against an ordinary enemy, step away, and trigger Overdrive deliberately. Watch for the slowdown so you know what a successful activation looks like on your setup. Next, test it while locked on, then cancel it manually and confirm the lock stays where you left it. Only once all three checks behave as expected should you carry the binding into a Nushi or boss attempt.

If you play on a controller, the same test is still worth running, because the October update also changed Guard to a continuous press-and-hold input. Your hands are now doing something different during defensive sequences than they were in August, and it is worth confirming that a stick press for Overdrive does not collide with how you now hold Guard.

## When Overdrive is worth the gauge

Because Overdrive empties the same resource that pays for guarding, Blade Arts and Nushi skills, it competes with your whole defensive and offensive kit. Polygon's guide recommends saving it for moments where slowed time clearly helps, such as dodging a tricky attack or making room to heal, and otherwise spending entanglement on the other abilities. That is the right default.

Good uses tend to share one trait: you already know what you want to do and only lack time. A red-flashing attack is one example. Polygon's guide explains that attacks with a red flash cannot be stopped by guard or parry and must be dodged, so slowed time can turn a panicked roll into a planned one. Another is a heal you keep getting punished for: trigger Overdrive, then heal, rather than gambling on a gap that never comes.

Poor uses share the opposite trait. Triggering Overdrive at the start of a fight, or as a reflex when health drops without a plan for the window, usually wastes the gauge and leaves you unable to guard the next string. One player in the Steam thread described the effect as lasting a very short time, which is a reason to have the follow-up action ready before you press it.

## Rebuilding the gauge afterward

After Overdrive, your next priority is getting entanglement back. Parries are the main source. Polygon also notes that the first chunk of the entanglement bar refills on its own even without parrying, which gives you a small cushion after emptying the bar. Some Koo mods return entanglement too; Polygon's example is Rapid Cooling, which recovers an entanglement point when you hit a burned enemy, pairing naturally with the flame-imbued Higanbana Bloom Art.

If Overdrive keeps leaving you exposed, practice parry timing first. The [parry, guard and dodge controls guide](/guides/parry-guard-dodge-controls-guide/) covers the inputs, and the [Guard and Rangifer timing guide](/guides/october-update-guard-rangifer-combat/) covers how the held Guard change affects defense after the October update. For the full list of what each patch changed, see the [v1.1.02 update guide](/guides/beast-reincarnation-v1-1-02-update-guide/) and the [v1.1.01 notes](/guides/beast-of-reincarnation-update-1-1-01/).

Treated as a short, deliberate slowdown rather than a super move, Overdrive becomes a dependable panic button. Bind it somewhere you cannot miss, confirm the activation once in a quiet area, and spend it only when you already know what the extra time is for.
