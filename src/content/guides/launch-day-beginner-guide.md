Beast of Reincarnation unlocked at **00:00 UTC on 4 August 2026** in every region, on the build Game Freak and Fictions labelled **version 1.0.6**. That means the guessing is over: the systems below are the ones in the shipped game.

This page is written from post-launch coverage by outlets that played the finished release, not from trailers — every non-obvious detail is attributed, and the [sources are listed at the bottom](#where-these-details-come-from). We have not personally cleared the game, so nothing here is presented as our own verified clear.

## The one thing to learn first: parrying feeds everything

Parrying is not just a defensive option in Beast of Reincarnation — it is the resource engine the rest of the kit runs on. Per Polygon's combat guide, a successful parry does four things at once:

- Negates the damage of the attack you parried.
- Grants **florescence points (FP)**, the resource for Koo's bloom arts.
- Grants **entanglement points**, needed to guard, to use blade arts and Nushi skills, and to trigger entanglement overdrive.
- Triggers your weapon's effect on Emma's blade.

The parry button is **Q** on PC and **LB / L1** on a controller, pressed just before the attack lands. Press it **too early** and it degrades into an ordinary guard — the same button, a worse outcome. A successful parry makes Emma's sword throw off yellow sparkles; a guard does not, which is the cleanest visual tell for whether your timing landed.

Guarding is a fallback, not a plan. It drains the **entanglement gauge** (the part-yellow, part-blue bar at the bottom of the screen), and once that bar is empty you take full damage even while holding guard. It also gives you no FP. After a successful parry, press attack for a **riposte**.

When an enemy **flashes red** as it winds up, the attack cannot be parried or guarded at all. Dodge instead — **Ctrl** on PC, **B** on Xbox, **Circle** on PlayStation — then press attack for a counterattack.

## Two bars, two purposes — do not confuse them

New players routinely conflate these. They are separate resources that happen to share one source (parrying).

| Resource | Spent on | How it refills |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Florescence points (FP)** | Koo's bloom arts | Successful parries, and Koo's own skill use |
| **Entanglement gauge** | Guarding, Emma's blade arts, Nushi skills, entanglement overdrive | Successful parries; the **first chunk only** also refills on its own |

That last detail matters: if you never parry successfully, FP stays empty, but Emma still regains one chunk of entanglement automatically. So a run built purely on dodging can still use cheap blade arts — it just locks Koo out of his best output.

## Koo's bloom arts, and the QTE nobody explains

Open Koo's bloom arts menu with **F** on PC, **Y** on Xbox, or **Triangle** on PlayStation. Time slows while the menu is open but **does not fully pause** — this is the "turn-based" half of the hybrid combat Game Freak advertised. Bloom arts do things like fire projectiles at enemies or pull them toward you.

Every bloom art includes a **quicktime event**. Hit the button while the marker is inside the yellow arc and Koo deals maximum damage; miss it and the attack still happens, just weaker. Three consequences worth memorising:

- A successful QTE restores some of **Emma's** HP. Bloom arts are a healing source, not only a damage source.
- A **double QTE** (two button presses) also returns some entanglement gauge to Emma.
- If FP is **full**, Koo gets the full bloom effect: **+100% damage and +50% stagger**.

You can chain different bloom arts back to back, but you **cannot use the same bloom art twice in a row**, so build a rotation rather than spamming a favourite.

**Mods** are the build layer. Each bloom art can take mods that add secondary effects — HP restoration, extra damage, and so on — bought and slotted from Koo's skill tree. ScreenRant notes the trees specialise: the **Incantation** side leans into building status effects faster, while the **Guard** side supports Emma directly. You are not locked into one tree, and once you meet the relevant NPC a little way into the story you can **respec** Koo's attributes and skills. Rest at a campsite or return to the Cleanse Walker to use the "acquire skills" menu.

For the complete input and timing sequence, continue with the [Koo Bloom Arts, FP and QTE guide](/guides/koo-bloom-arts-qte-guide/).

Outside of bloom arts you do not need to micromanage Koo — he uses his regular abilities on his own. Kotaku's advice is worth repeating: when Koo peels off to fight something you were not targeting, let him. You *can* order an attack on an unaware enemy, with **right mouse + middle mouse** on PC or **LT/L2 + right stick** on a controller.

## Emma's blade arts and Nushi skills

Emma has two families of special attacks, both unlocked by story progress: **blade arts** and **Nushi skills**. Both are essentially melee combos, and both cost entanglement — you lose one "chunk" of the bar when you press the final input in the sequence. Only **blade arts** can be upgraded in Emma's skill tree, and those points can be respecced.

To read the inputs, open the equipment menu and choose "view skills" at the bottom of Emma's page. Do not try to learn the whole list at once. Polygon recommends starting with **Shakuyaku** and **Climbing Higanbana**, which need only one and two buttons respectively.

## Stagger, daze, and free executions

Under an enemy's red HP bar is a **yellow stagger bar** that fills on every successful hit *or* parry. Fill it on a normal enemy and it is stunned for a few seconds — press attack for an **execution** that kills it outright, regardless of remaining HP. This is why aggressive parrying shortens fights so dramatically.

The exception: the game's heavyweights, **Nushi** included, cannot be stunned. Filling their stagger bar **dazes** them instead — no execution prompt, but several seconds of free damage.

The focused [elements, weakness, stagger and daze guide](/guides/elements-weakness-stagger-guide/) separates elemental matching from the yellow stagger bar and explains how to test both without invented values.

## Elements, and matching Emma to Koo

There are **four elemental effects** — **poison, acid, flame, lightning** — all of which deal damage over time. Alongside them sit two utility damage types: **destructive** attacks break enemy shields, and **bind** attacks restrict movement.

An enemy's weakness is displayed next to its HP bar. Open Koo's bloom art menu and the word **"weak"** is stamped over any art that matches it, so you never have to guess mid-fight.

Emma's attacks and weapon can also be imbued with an element, and buildup is faster when **both** halves of the duo push the same one — a real reason to memorise which blade art and Nushi skill combos pair with your current bloom arts. Mods interact with this too: Polygon's example is running the flame-imbued Higanbana bloom art for burn, then equipping the **Rapid Cooling** mod, which returns an entanglement point whenever you hit a burning enemy.

## Healing, status effects, and hunger

- **Stimpacks** are your in-combat heal. You carry **three** by default; **dragon veil shrines** raise that cap.
- **Emergency medicine** is the easy-to-miss one. Open Koo's bloom arts menu and press the heal button (**L2** on PS5, for example) to heal *and* clear status effects at once. Field stock is finite. ScreenRant notes a later mod, **Healing Energy**, that cures Emma's status effects when Koo uses the matching bloom art.
- **Campfires** restore HP, stimpacks and emergency medicine — but they **respawn every enemy in the area**. Treat resting as a trade.
- **Hunger** is a passive system: as long as the hunger meter is not empty, Emma and Koo slowly regain HP out of combat with no campfire needed. Check it in the backpack (**C** on PC, **down** on controller).

## Entanglement overdrive is not a super move

This is the mechanic most likely to disappoint you if you misread it. With the entanglement gauge full, hold **Shift + middle mouse** on PC or press **both sticks** on a controller to trigger **entanglement overdrive**. It **slows time for a few seconds and nothing else** — there is no special attack attached.

Because it consumes the bar, it competes directly with guarding, blade arts and Nushi skills. Save it for a specific problem: a telegraph you keep failing to dodge, or a window you need to heal in.

## Traversal is a combat tool, not just navigation

Emma's blight-stricken braid has two abilities you should master immediately:

- **Lift** — hold the jump button to rise, functioning like an extended double jump.
- **Extend** — grow her hair forward in a straight line to bridge gaps.

Both are movement options on paper and **opening moves** in practice. Extend a bridge over an enemy, walk out onto it and drop for an **air assassination**; or use Lift to climb above an enemy's awareness range and fall on it. Crouching and approaching from behind works too. Kotaku makes the wider point that the level design consistently offers somewhere to climb to, so a fight you are not ready for is usually a fight you can decline — and height is also an excellent place to shoot from.

The [Lift and Extend traversal guide](/guides/lift-extend-traversal-guide/) expands this into a route-reading checklist for gaps, high ledges, campsites, Koo discoveries, and water boundaries.

Two blunt limits: Emma **cannot swim**, so fleeing into water just deposits you back on the shore with the enemies still waiting; and the environment does not care that you are outnumbered.

For ranged work Emma carries both a **bow** and a **crossbow**, swapped with **1** and **2** on PC or the **left/right** arrows on a controller. The crossbow fires quickly and suits live combat; the bow needs windup and suits sniping something that has not noticed you.

## What to prioritise in your first few hours

1. **Take the dodge roll early.** Emma's starting side-step has a tight window and barely moves her. Kotaku recommends pushing down the **Tracking Shot** path on Emma's skill tree to unlock a full dodge roll, and treating it as a priority rather than a nice-to-have.
2. **Find the campsite before you touch the boss.** Most boss arenas have one nearby for resting, saving and applying upgrades, but not all are obvious. Locate it first or you fight without a checkpoint.
3. **Explore for Cleanse Walker materials.** Weapon and projectile upgrades are gated behind your base's level, and levelling the **Cleanse Walker** needs **Generator Devices, Electronic Brains and Memory Chips** found across each region. The equipment enhancement screen shows the base level a weapon needs next, so sweeping regions is a progression requirement, not optional completionism.
4. **Go home on purpose.** Resting at the Walker restocks supplies, lets you check crops, and lets Koo and your young companion **Kagura** run research for more items — plus character scenes. Any campsite menu flags in the bottom-right corner when a companion has new dialogue.
5. **Let enemies fight each other.** Wild factions do brawl; wait it out and you inherit one weakened group instead of two healthy ones.

## Difficulty and language settings you should check now

The Steam listing carries an **Adjustable Difficulty** tag, and post-launch coverage names three settings: **Story**, **Normal** and **Hard**, switchable **on the fly**. Normal is a conventional Soulslike challenge where a few hits kill you. Story significantly reduces incoming damage — Kotaku's caveat is that it is not a free ride: you still need to parry, dodge and exploit weaknesses, but it sharply cuts deaths against hard bosses.

One genuinely buried setting: the game ships with **both Japanese and English full audio**, but the English track has to be selected from the **main menu**, not the in-game pause menu, and nothing prompts you about it. Kotaku's reviewer only discovered it during the credits. If you would rather not read subtitles during fights, change it before you start.
